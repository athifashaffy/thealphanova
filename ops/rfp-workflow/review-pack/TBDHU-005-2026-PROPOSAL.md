## 1. Understanding of the Assessment

### 1.1 What the Health Unit is buying

The Thunder Bay District Health Unit is buying a needs assessment for the replacement of its public website. It is not buying a website. The successful proponent studies how the current site of roughly 520 pages serves a district of about 160,000 people across a large geography, works with staff at the Health Unit's five sites, engages the public and partners, and produces the evidence, requirements and specification the Health Unit will use to run a separate procurement for the build. The RFP states that the proponent awarded this assessment cannot bid on the later website build. We accept that condition without reservation; Section 6 sets out how we protect the Health Unit's independence throughout. `[ATHIF TO CONFIRM FROM OFFICIAL PDF: exact scope statement and deliverable list]`

### 1.2 What this engagement is not

It is not a redesign, a content rewrite or a CMS selection dressed up as an assessment. We will not recommend a named platform unless the Health Unit asks for a category-level options analysis, and even then it will be presented as options with trade-offs, not as a choice made for the Health Unit. It is not an accessibility remediation of the current site, though the assessment will audit the current site's accessibility to inform the requirements.

### 1.3 The go / no-go we will not paper over

The Alpha Nova has not delivered a website needs assessment for an Ontario public health unit. We have delivered web and mobile products for a public college and a university hospital, we practise AODA and WCAG accessibility, and our team is in Northern Ontario and understands the distances involved between Thunder Bay, Marathon, Nipigon, Geraldton and Manitouwadge. We would rather the evaluators see that gap here than find it in our references.

### 1.4 What matters most for this site

**Public health information has to be found, not browsed.** A parent looking up immunization clinics, a restaurant operator checking inspection requirements, a person in crisis looking for a number, none of them will navigate 520 pages. The assessment has to measure how people actually arrive and search, and translate that into an information architecture and search requirement.

**Accessibility is a legal obligation, not a preference.** As a designated public sector organisation under the AODA, the Health Unit's website must meet WCAG 2.0 Level AA. The later build RFP will carry that requirement; this assessment must give it teeth by documenting the current site's conformance, identifying content types that routinely fail (PDF forms, embedded maps, videos without captions), and writing testable acceptance criteria. We will also note where WCAG 2.1 and 2.2 success criteria are practical to require so the Health Unit is not procuring to a standard that is already behind.

**Privacy and trust.** Health information services collect personal health information through forms, appointment bookings and reporting tools. PHIPA and MFIPPA obligations shape what the site may collect, where it may be hosted, and how consent and retention work. These are requirements for the build; the assessment has to surface them.

**Plain language and language access.** Public health content must be readable by people with a wide range of literacy levels, and French-language and Indigenous-language considerations in the district must be assessed rather than assumed. `[ATHIF TO CONFIRM with TBDHU during discovery: French Language Services Act obligations and any Indigenous language commitments]`

**Staff who publish.** Program areas at five sites publish content today with whatever training and governance exists. The assessment must understand that workflow, its bottlenecks and its ownership gaps, because the next CMS will be judged by whether a nurse can post a clinic change in five minutes.

**Emergency communication.** Public health sites carry outbreak notices, boil-water advisories, heat and smoke alerts. Surge capacity, emergency banner workflows and integration with the Health Unit's social and alerting channels are requirements the assessment must capture.

### 1.5 Outcomes

By 15 May 2027 the Health Unit will hold an evidence base (analytics, search, survey and interview findings), a content inventory and audit of all 520 pages with keep, merge, rewrite or retire decisions proposed, an accessibility and privacy baseline, a recommended information architecture, functional and non-functional requirements, a category-level options analysis, an implementation roadmap and a build RFP specification the Health Unit can issue with light editing.

## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 Principles

1. Evidence before opinion: analytics, search logs, surveys and interviews before any recommendation.
2. Users first: the public, then partners, then publishers, then IT.
3. Accessibility and privacy as requirements with acceptance tests, not as a chapter.
4. Vendor-neutral: category-level options, transparent scoring, no named recommendation unless asked.
5. Procurement-ready output: the Health Unit should be able to issue the build RFP from our deliverables with light editing.

### 3.2 Streams

**Stream A. Evidence and analytics.** Twelve months of web analytics and internal search terms; top entry pages and dead ends; device and browser mix, which in the district skews to mobile; peak events (outbreaks, advisories); referral sources including social channels.

**Stream B. Public and partner engagement.** An online survey promoted through the Health Unit's channels and at the five sites, targeting a statistically useful sample across the district; six to eight moderated remote usability sessions on the current site with recruited residents including people who use assistive technology; short structured interviews with partner organisations (municipalities, school boards, primary care, community agencies) `[ATHIF TO CONFIRM FROM OFFICIAL PDF: any prescribed engagement scope]`.

**Stream C. Internal engagement.** Interviews and two workshops with program-area publishers, communications, privacy, IT and leadership across the five sites (remote by default, one in-person day in Thunder Bay proposed, see Section 4). Publishing workflow mapping: who writes, who approves, how long it takes, what breaks.

**Stream D. Content inventory and audit.** Automated crawl of all approximately 520 pages plus documents; manual audit of each page for ownership, currency, traffic, readability grade, duplication and accessibility issues; proposed disposition (keep, merge, rewrite, retire) for Health Unit decision.

**Stream E. Accessibility and privacy baseline.** Automated WCAG scan of the full site and manual testing of representative templates and top-traffic pages against WCAG 2.0 AA, with notes on 2.1 and 2.2 criteria; PDF and media audit; review of forms and tools that collect personal or personal health information against PHIPA and MFIPPA; hosting and data-location review.

**Stream F. Requirements, IA and options.** Recommended information architecture and navigation model validated with card-sorting and tree-testing; functional and non-functional requirements tagged Must / Should / Could, including CMS publishing, search, emergency communication, multilingual support, integrations (clinic booking, inspection disclosure, forms), security, hosting, performance and support; category-level options analysis (open-source CMS, commercial SaaS CMS, proprietary agency platform; hosted in Canada) scored on criteria the Health Unit sets; rough-order-of-magnitude effort bands for the build presented as ranges with basis stated, in the pricing envelope only if the RFP requires them there, otherwise in the final report without dollar figures in this technical file.

**Stream G. Build RFP specification.** Requirements, acceptance criteria, evaluation criteria suggestions, WCAG 2.0 AA conformance and testing clauses, privacy and hosting clauses, content migration expectations and a governance model, written so the Health Unit's procurement staff can issue it.

### 3.3 Quality controls

Every finding cites its evidence. Requirements are reviewed by a team member who did not run the interview. The options scoring workbook is delivered with formulas visible. Accessibility findings are reproducible with the tool, browser and assistive technology named.
